# Growth Strategy

Tactics without strategy is a to-do list. Use this skill to decide **where** growth
comes from before deciding **what** to build. Work top-down: Fits → engine (loops) →
one metric to steer by.

## 1. The Four Fits (Brian Balfour)

Sustainable growth past ~$100M requires four fits to *interlock*, not just
product-market fit alone. A break in any one caps the whole system.

1. **Market ↔ Product fit** — the product serves a real, sized market.
2. **Product ↔ Channel fit** — the product is shaped so a channel can distribute it
   (you build for the channel; you don't bend the channel to the product).
3. **Channel ↔ Model fit** — the channel's economics match your monetization (a
   $10/mo product cannot afford a high-touch sales channel; a $100k ACV product
   cannot grow on cheap self-serve virality alone).
4. **Model ↔ Market fit** — your pricing/ARPU × market size can actually reach the
   revenue goal.

**How to use it:** when growth stalls, don't just optimize the funnel — check which
fit broke. New channel not working? Probably product-channel fit, not the channel.

## 2. Growth loops beat funnels

A funnel is linear and leaks: you pour traffic in the top, some converts, and you
must keep re-buying the top. A **growth loop** is a closed system where the output of
one cycle becomes the input of the next, so growth compounds.

Loop shape: `Action → Output → Reinvestment → more of the same Action`.

Loop types:
- **Viral / word-of-mouth loops** — users invite users (see `referral-viral-loops`).
- **Content loops** — users/UGC create content → indexed/shared → attracts users who
  create more content (e.g. marketplaces, Q&A sites, Pinterest).
- **Paid loops** — revenue from new users funds ads that acquire more users (only a
  loop if LTV > CAC with a fast enough payback).
- **Sales loops** — usage/expansion revenue funds sales capacity that lands more
  accounts.

**Rule:** identify your *one or two* primary loops and make them faster and stronger.
Most companies have one dominant loop that drives 60–80% of growth.

## 3. The Racecar framework (Lenny/Reforge mental model)

Separate the parts so you invest in the right one:
- **Engine = growth loops** — the compounding core. Biggest long-term leverage.
- **Lubricants = optimizations** — conversion-rate, onboarding, funnel tuning. Make
  the engine run smoother, but don't create new growth on their own.
- **Fuel = funnels / paid** — real but not self-sustaining; you keep buying it.
- **Turbo boosts = one-off tactics** — launches, PR, partnerships. Spiky, not durable.

Misdiagnosis is the trap: teams pour effort into lubricants and one-off boosts and
wonder why growth doesn't compound. Build the engine first.

## 4. North Star Metric (NSM)

One metric that captures the value delivered to customers **and** predicts durable
revenue. It is behavioral, not financial, and not a vanity count.

Good NSM tests:
- Does moving it reliably lead to revenue/retention *later*?
- Does it represent a customer getting real value (not just a click)?
- Can teams actually influence it with product/GTM work?

Examples: nights booked (Airbnb), messages sent (a chat app), weekly active
collaborators (a design tool). **Not** signups, pageviews, or revenue itself.

Pair the NSM with 3–5 **input metrics** (levers teams own) and monitor **output
metrics** (revenue, retention). See `plg-metrics` for the tree.

## Strategy checklist

- [ ] Which of the Four Fits is strongest? Which is the current bottleneck?
- [ ] What is the ONE primary growth loop? Draw it as a closed cycle.
- [ ] Are we investing in the engine, or just lubricants and turbo boosts?
- [ ] Is our NSM behavioral, value-capturing, and predictive of revenue?
- [ ] Do we have 3–5 input levers mapped to teams?

## Common traps

- Treating PMF as one fit instead of four → scaling a channel the model can't afford.
- Optimizing a leaky funnel instead of building a loop.
- Picking a vanity NSM (signups, revenue) that doesn't represent delivered value.
- Copying another company's loop; loops are specific to product + channel + model.
